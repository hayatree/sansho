# Native silicon B4SOI 4.4

Sansho accepts NMOS and PMOS model cards with `level=10` or `level=58` and an optional `version=4.4`. R17 implements operating point, nested DC and temperature sweeps, quasi-static AC, noise and transient analysis. The equations come from the UC Berkeley *BSIMSOIv4.4 Users’ Manual* (2010). The terminal conventions and level aliases also follow the ngspice 44 user manual §§7.6.1 and 7.6.4. Numeric defaults use the supplied 44.2 parameter listing where available. A missing value in that listing is distinct from a measured zero. Eleven of the twelve R17 acceptance decks pass against the cached ngspice 44.2 waveforms. The coarse-grid CMOS inverter is a known difference. Its 45 fF follow-up passes on the 20 ps grid.

## Connecting a device

The first four pins are drain, front gate, source and back gate. The back gate contacts the substrate across the buried oxide. Appendix A.2 defines the remaining pins.

| Pins after the back gate | Instance flag | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| none | none | Floating film |
| P | none | Body contact through the model body resistance |
| P B | none | Body contact and separately accessible film |
| T | `tnodeout` | Floating film and accessible temperature rise |
| P T | `tnodeout` | Body contact and temperature rise |
| P B T | `tnodeout` | Body contact, accessible film and temperature rise |

For example, the following original card exposes the floating film's temperature through node `tj`. With self-heating active, one volt on that pin represents one kelvin above the device ambient temperature. A zero body-contact resistance ties P and B directly. An explicit ground on P fixes the contact potential. Omitting P allows a partially depleted film to find its own potential.

```spice
M1 drain gate 0 back tj film l=.48u w=2.4u tnodeout
.model film nmos(level=10 version=4.4 soimod=0 tox=4.6n tsi=75n
+ tbox=105n nch=1.1e17 k1=.42 vth0=.48 shmod=1 rth0=.036 cth0=.00012)
```

`soiMod=0` solves the partially depleted film current balance. Mode 1 adds the back-gate-dependent lower bound for a device whose depletion changes with bias (unified PD/FD). Mode 2 uses the ideal fully depleted electrostatic solution and suppresses neutral-film current and charge. Mode 3 chooses among these at binding using film thickness, doping and the `VBS0PD` and `VBS0FD` thresholds. The instance can override the model selector. The older level 55–57 models have distinct card interfaces.

## Equations and implementation

| Manual section | Quantities evaluated |
|---|---|
| §§2.1–2.5, Appendix C | Equations 2.1–2.5 limit forward film bias and continue the threshold surface potential. The film thickness enters the bulk charge coefficient in Eq. 2.6. Eq. 2.7 combines the BSIM3-type channel with velocity saturation, series resistance and output modulation. The fit includes the v4 pocket shift and mobility choices 1–4. |
| §§3.1–3.6 | Source and drain each have diffusion, recombination, tunnelling and neutral-film injection. The lateral bipolar current couples the two junctions. Impact generation injects current into the film, providing feedback to the threshold. `IIIMOD`, `GIDLMOD`, `IGCMOD` and `IGBMOD` (alias `IGMOD`) select the impact, induced leakage and oxide current laws. |
| Chapter 4 | Eqs. 4.1–4.14 determine quasi-static inversion, accumulation, depletion, partition and back-gate charge. `CAPMOD=2/3` selects classical/finite-centroid capacitance. Eqs. 4.15–4.19 add junction storage, bias-dependent bottom capacitance, overlap and contact charge. Each branch moves equal and opposite charge between its endpoints. |
| §§5.1–5.2 | The junction current laws, mobility, saturation velocity and threshold depend on temperature. The thermal residual uses channel plus bipolar dissipation. Eqs. 5.2–5.3 scale resistance inversely and heat capacity directly with thermal width. An external network on T participates in AC and transient as well as DC. |
| Chapter 6 | `FDMOD=0/1` selects front/back electrostatic coupling. The second form includes drain coupling through `CDSBS`. The ideal-FD film bending limit also adjusts the effective channel doping. |
| Chapters 7 and 9 | `RGATEMOD=1` adds electrode resistance, mode 2 adds a bias-dependent gate conductance and mode 3 splits the gate and overlap connection. `RBODYMOD` provides separate drain-side and source-side film nodes. `RDSMOD=1` exposes the asymmetric access resistances. Gate-to-isolation distances use the shared Sansho length-of-diffusion stress calculation. |
| Chapter 8 | `FNOIMOD=0/1` selects empirical or trap-density flicker noise. `TNOIMOD=0/1/2` selects inversion-charge, partitioned RF or transconductance-based thermal noise. The RF source-voltage generator enters the circuit through the source column of the current and charge derivatives. Branch generators account for resistors, the two film junctions and gate current. |
| Appendices A, B and E | Effective electrical and charge dimensions are separate. Eligible coefficients use inverse effective length, inverse per-finger width and their product. `BINUNIT` controls their units. Drawn width, finger count, segmentation, body-contact geometry and instance multiplier enter the corresponding current, charge and thermal areas. |

The charge partition integral, nonlinear overlap, direct-tunnelling barrier evaluation and layout stress reuse existing Sansho BSIM4 code. The SOI film and buried-oxide terms have separate derivatives. AC and transient use the same terminal current and charge Jacobians, including their temperature column.

The `@m1[...]` syntax exposes intrinsic `id`, `gm`, `gds`, `gmbs`, `vth` and `vdsat`, terminal charges `qd/qg/qs/qb` and their four-by-four derivatives. SOI quantities include `vbs`, `ves`, `ids` (channel plus lateral collector), `ic`, `ibs/ibd`, `isub`, `igidl/igisl`, gate-current components and `qbs/qbd`. A voltage source in series with an external terminal measures total lead current, including displacement current in AC and transient. Appendix A lists the instance IC tuple in drain, gate, film, back-gate and contact order. In the R17 UIC sweep all three tuples yield the same film history. A nodal .ic sets the initial nodal voltage.

## R17b measurements against 44.2

The comparisons use the original decks, the supplied parameter listing and the cached ngspice raw files. Scores divide the largest absolute difference by the comparison tolerance. One is the pass boundary. Parameter rejection is counted separately from waveform agreement.

| Manual law | Extraction and numerical check |
|---|---|
| Film potential, Eqs. 6.1–6.9 and Appendix C1 | Evaluate the FD reference threshold with the same short-channel, swing and body laws used in transport, at the zero-field film potential for a surface potential of twice the bulk Fermi potential. The depletion drop includes the pocket factor `1 + LPE0/Leff`. In Eq. 6.8 the denominator is `MOINFD × K1 × Vt²`. Inverting the transfer sweeps gives about 0.281 V² for the .42 K1 card at 27 °C. The K1B and TBOX sweeps place a 20.0 mV addition after the divider. The negative back-bias data resolve a 5 mV join in the quadratic zero-field correction. |
| Depletion mode | The unified film follows the upper of its physical voltage and the electrostatic floor. The floor sweep gives a 10 mV shift and a 10 mV square-root smoothing scale. Under FDMOD=1 the three CDSBS sweeps retain the front and drain weights of Eq. 6.4 in the zero-back-field potential. At 150 nm the oracle prints a clipped doping of 5.8623 × 10¹⁶ cm⁻³. Substitution into §6.1 gives a silicon gap of 1.1200 eV. |
| PD threshold, Eqs. 2.1–2.6 | The lower film limiter has a 1 mV width. The upper two use 2 mV and the limiting voltage in the radicand. The contacted and floating sweeps resolve K2 = −0.0186 when only K1 is specified. With injection disabled the numerical shunts between gate and drain and between film and source measure GMIN × 10⁻⁶. |
| Gate tunnelling, Eqs. 3.12–3.25 | Differencing log current between two AIGC settings recovers oxide drops of 0.43162, 0.64004 and 1.04460 V at gate biases 0.6, 0.8 and 1.2 V. | Charge, Eqs. 4.1, 4.10–4.17 and Appendix C | With gate drive, changing FBODY gives a film slope of 1.826 fF and a drain slope of opposite sign with magnitude 1.660 fF per unit FBODY. | Charge reporting and startup, Appendix A | The 56-point standard DC KETA sweeps report zero terminal charge for every setting. Appendix A.3 specifies DEBUG=−1 for charge calculation in a DC sweep. At that bias the film charge is approximately −1.88 fC, the stored charge at zero terminal bias. Sampling the initial nodal voltages reproduces the subsequent 214-point histories. |
| Noise, Eqs. 8.7–8.11 and §8.3 | Inversion charge for the channel thermal generator precedes terminal allocation of depletion charge. The RF series generator acts through a distinct source access node. The 0, 25 and 100 Ω sweeps resolve this condition. NOIF scales the separated film-junction shot generators. Integrated output noise with NOIF=0 agrees within 0.02 µV on the impact card, while its input-referred spectrum and the finite-NOIF spectra still differ. |

Appendix A instance keywords are bound separately from model parameters. An unknown instance key produces an elaboration error. The optional resistance coefficients RBDB/RBSB and RDW/RSW are recognized even with their networks inactive. Three 44.2 logs explicitly reject instance MULU0. Sansho reports `E-ELAB-PARAM`. The mode-four mobility sweep follows the Appendix B EU default of zero, reproducing drain current to within 0.2 nA. A separate explicit-EU sweep tests field dependence.

### Inverter acceptance

The original 45 fF deck requests 80 ps and runs for 17 ns. The 45 fF follow-up uses the same devices, pulse and load, requests 20 ps, raises the Newton iteration limit and runs for 7 ns. At 5.436738 ns the coarse ngspice reference gives 67.427 mV. Linear interpolation between the fine ngspice values at 5.420 and 5.440 ns gives 39.749 mV. Their **27.678 mV** difference exceeds the **12.48 mV** acceptance allowance. Even the upper fine-grid endpoint is only 47.744 mV. Changing a device parameter to match this edge would confound the charge law with the choice of accepted timepoints. [Acceptance deck](../verify/decks/r17_soi_inverter.cir) · [Load sweep](../verify/probes/r17b_inverter_film.cir)

On the coarse deck, the remaining maximum differences are 29.56 mV in output and 23.62 µA in supply current. On the 20 ps grid all eight recorded quantities pass at both 45 and 90 fF. At 45 fF the output differs by at most 5.11 mV. At 20 fF the output passes, while the PMOS film and supply current differ by up to 18.1 mV and 27.2 µA. The coarse deck retains its original tolerance and a known-difference annotation.

A diagnostic replay prescribed only the cached timepoints. Sansho still solved its own terminal voltages, charges and currents. On the coarse grid it reproduced the first 90 of 245 samples through 5.599115 ns within 2.66 mV and 1.75 µA. Newton did not converge on the next prescribed 80 ps step at 5.679115 ns with the deck’s ten-iteration limit. The 20 and 45 fF follow-ups also required a step reduction when forced onto their oracle grids, at 1.38 and 1.54 ns respectively. The 90 fF replay completed all 369 points. This experiment separates the error from timepoint selection on the completed intervals. The acceptance counts use ordinary adaptive runs.

R17 covers the silicon material system. The optional Chapter 10 material equations, model-specific debug-output generation and SOA voltage monitoring remain outside the implementation. Supplied parameters without an implemented mapping produce `W-MODEL-IGNORED`. Invalid selector values, versions and invalid effective geometries produce `E-ELAB-MODEL`. The [probe index](../verify/probes/README.md#r17-b4soi-44) describes the native and cached-reference runs.

## Regression tests

Unit tests check charge and current conservation, central differences of both Jacobians with each electrical and thermal terminal perturbed, the limiting depletion modes, pin allocation and coefficient binning. An engine regression drives a floating device through gate turn-off with zero diffusion current. A 257-device level-58 circuit exercises parallel assembly and compares drain-current sample bits at one and two workers.
